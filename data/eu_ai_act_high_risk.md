# EU AI Act: High-Risk AI System Obligations

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) imposes strict obligations on providers of high-risk AI systems. Annex III lists high-risk use cases, including biometric identification, safety components of critical infrastructure, education and employment screening, access to essential services, law enforcement, migration management, and the administration of justice.

# Risk Management System

Article 9 requires providers to establish, implement, document and maintain a risk management system throughout the entire lifecycle of the high-risk AI system. The system must identify and analyse known and reasonably foreseeable risks to health, safety and fundamental rights, estimate and evaluate those risks, and adopt targeted risk management measures. Risk management must be tested and reviewed on an ongoing basis, and it must address risks that arise from the interaction between the AI system and its operating environment.

# Data Governance

Article 10 requires that training, validation and testing data sets be relevant, sufficiently representative, and as free of errors and complete as possible in view of the intended purpose. Providers must implement data governance and management practices covering design choices, data collection processes and the origin of data, relevant data preparation processing operations such as annotation, labelling, cleaning, updating, enrichment and aggregation, the formulation of relevant assumptions, and the evaluation of data set suitability and possible biases. Data sets should take into account the geographical, contextual, behavioural and functional setting in which the system will be used.

# Technical Documentation

Article 11 requires providers to draw up and keep up to date technical documentation demonstrating compliance with the Act's requirements before the system is placed on the market. Annex IV specifies the documentation contents: a general description of the AI system and its intended purpose, a detailed description of the elements of the system and the development process (including training methodologies, data sets used, and human oversight measures), information on monitoring, functioning and control of the system, and a description of the risk management system.

# Logging and Record-Keeping

Article 12 requires high-risk AI systems to technically enable the automatic recording of events ("logs") throughout the system's lifetime. The logging capabilities must ensure a level of traceability of the system's functioning appropriate to the intended purpose, enabling the identification of situations that may lead to risks, post-market monitoring obligations under Article 72, and monitoring of operations referred to in Article 26(5). Providers must retain logs for an appropriate period for their control of compliance with the Act.

# Human Oversight

Article 14 requires that high-risk AI systems be designed and developed so that natural persons can effectively oversee them. Oversight measures must enable the individuals assigned to it to understand the system's capabilities and limitations, remain aware of the possible tendency of automation bias, correctly interpret the system's output, and be able to decide, in any particular situation, not to use the system or otherwise disregard, override or reverse its output. For certain biometric identification systems, oversight requires verification and confirmation by at least two natural persons with the necessary competence, training and authority.

# Conformity Assessment

Before placing a high-risk AI system on the market, providers must carry out a conformity assessment under Article 43, either through internal control or with the involvement of a notified body, depending on the use case. Upon successful assessment, the provider draws up an EU declaration of conformity (Article 47) and affixes the CE marking (Article 48). Providers must also register the system in the EU database established under Article 71, and maintain post-market monitoring and incident reporting systems, including reporting serious incidents to market surveillance authorities within 15 days of becoming aware of them.
